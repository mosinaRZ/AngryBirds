# Angry Birds

**English** | [فارسی](README.fa.md)

An Angry Birds-style physics game for Android, built with Kotlin, libGDX and Box2D.
Drag the bird back (a short dotted arc shows where it will go), release, and bring the
structures down on the pigs. One handcrafted level is playable today, inside a campaign
tower that is ready for more.

The pigs' heads show a custom face image: replace one file (`enemy_head.png`) and the face
changes, no code change needed.

Private build, not published.

## Gameplay

- **5 birds per attempt**, fired one after another in the order given by the level:
  blue, yellow, blue, red, red (the `birds` list in `Levels.kt`, generated by `tools/generate_level.py`).
- **5 pigs** (circle bodies with HP) hide inside glass towers, a structure with TNT crates and a stone
  cage on wooden stilts on a tall rock. The level is laid out like the reference screenshot
  (100 px = 1 m); terrain pieces are real static bodies.
- Pull the bird back from the slingshot (clamped to 1.9 m), release to fire. The launch speed follows
  the pull length.
- **Birds and abilities:** red (plain), yellow (tap while flying to dash), blue (tap while flying to split
  in three). Abilities only work before the bird has touched anything. The flight trail marks the place
  where an ability was used with a small badge, and a split draws all three paths.
- **Damage** comes from the collision impulse. Blocks and pigs lose HP and are removed at 0 HP.
  TNT explodes: blocks close to the blast shatter, farther pieces are thrown away.
- **Win:** every pig is destroyed. The level then ends once the score has not changed for about 2.4 s.
  **Lose:** no birds left and everything has been still for 2 seconds.
- **Score:** points per destroyed pig and block (a small "+points" number appears at each one),
  10,000 per unused bird.
- **Stars:** clearing the level = 1 star, 35,000 points = 2 stars, 48,000 points = 3 stars
  (saving birds is what earns them). Best score and best stars are saved on the device.
- The path of the last shot stays on screen as a white dotted trail and is wiped when the next bird leaves
  the slingshot.

### HUD (top right while playing)

- **Score** in big gold digits; it counts up instead of jumping and swells when a bonus number arrives.
- **Pig bar:** fills as pigs are defeated (one slice per pig). The **counter inside the bar** shows
  defeated / total (for example `2/5`) and turns gold when the last pig is down.
- **Score bar:** fills with the score. Three star markers sit at the star thresholds and light up with a
  pop. On this bar the stars are **bronze (first), silver (second) and gold (third)**, and the bar takes the
  color of the last star reached.
- **Pause** (round button, top left): freezes the game and offers Resume, Restart, Menu and, for both music and
  sound FX, a round mute button and a volume slider (the same settings as the lobby).

### Level end

The camera zooms onto the winners and they celebrate with confetti. When the level is won, every unused
bird cheers where it stands (slingshot, bench or ground) and jumps in turn; at the top of its jump a
"+10000" bursts out and flies in an arc to the score. Then the result panel appears with three big stars
(bronze on the left, gold in the middle and bigger, silver on the right; they appear one after the other as bronze, silver, gold), the score and best score counting up,
and Restart / Menu buttons. The final score is shown big. Each earned star lands with a flash and gets a soft colored
halo, two layers of rays turning in opposite directions and a few twinkling stars around it.

### Menus and flow

`splash -> lobby (theme song) -> level tower -> level`

- **Splash:** the title artwork slowly pushes in while a loading bar fills with a little bird rolling along
  its end. A tap skips the wait after a moment.
- **Lobby:** the artwork (`sp_bg.png`) fills the screen and breathes slightly, with a soft shade at the
  bottom, drifting sparkles, a floating "Continue campaign" button that glows now and then, your best score
  in a pill at the bottom left (after the first finished level) and a round **settings button** at the
  top right.
- **Settings** (gear button in the lobby): two rows, each with a round icon button and a volume slider with the
  percent next to it. Everything is saved on the device.
  - **Music:** the lobby, level tower and in-game music. Drag the slider to set the volume (50% plays the music at
    half its normal level). Tapping the icon mutes the music completely (the icon is dimmed with a red slash and the
    slider turns grey) and tapping it again brings it back at the same volume. Muting fades the track out; unmuting
    fades it back in.
  - **Sound FX:** every sound effect, including button clicks, bird voices and touch feedback, with the same slider and
    icon. Releasing the slider plays a click so the new level can be heard.
  - Moving a slider while its row is muted unmutes it; tapping the icon to unmute while the volume is 0% restores 50%.
  The same two rows also sit in the **pause panel** during play and use the same saved settings.
  The panel closes with its X button, with a tap outside it, or with the Android Back button.
- **Level tower:** a scrollable tower of themed rooms with arched doors (done / next / padlocked). 14 doors
  exist (3 + 3 + 4 + 4); only level 1 is open, the others shake with a dull sound when tapped.
- **Single window:** the manifest forbids split screen and floating windows.

### Audio

Bird voices (launch and idle chirps), ability sounds, collision and break sounds per material, a looping
menu track and a calm outdoor ambience during play (see [ASSETS.md](ASSETS.md) for what is bundled and how
to replace it). The sound is stopped when the app goes to the background. Music and sound FX each have their own
volume (0 to 100%, default 100%) and their own mute button, see Settings above.

### Controls

Touch only. Volume sliders are dragged with a finger (a tap on the track jumps there). In the lobby the Android Back button closes the settings panel, or leaves the game when the
panel is closed. In a level it toggles pause. Tapping the top-right corner of the screen during play
toggles the Box2D debug overlay (useful for tuning, off by default).

## Build and run

Requirements: Android Studio with JDK 17, an Android device or emulator (API 24+).

1. Open the project root in Android Studio and let Gradle sync.
2. Run the `android` configuration on a device.

Command line (from the project root):

```
./gradlew assembleDebug        # APK: android/build/outputs/apk/debug/android-debug.apk
./gradlew installDebug         # build and install on the connected device
./gradlew assembleRelease      # APK: android/build/outputs/apk/release/
```

A release APK is signed only if `keystore.properties` exists in the project root
(see the comment in `android/build.gradle` for its four keys). Without it, Gradle
produces an unsigned release APK, which Android will not install; use the debug
APK for sharing, or create a keystore once:

```
keytool -genkeypair -v -keystore release.jks -alias slingshot -keyalg RSA -keysize 2048 -validity 10000
```

### Build files

The Gradle files (`build.gradle`, `settings.gradle`, `gradle.properties`,
`core/build.gradle`, `android/build.gradle`) and the Android module files
(`AndroidManifest.xml`, `AndroidLauncher.kt`, `res/`) are a reference scaffold
that follows the usual gdx-liftoff layout. They have not been built in the
environment that produced this delivery (no network, no Android SDK).
If you already have a working project generated by gdx-liftoff, keep your own
Gradle files and versions and only copy `core/` and `android/assets/` into it.

Versions in `gradle.properties` (libGDX 1.13.1, Kotlin 2.0.21, Android Gradle
Plugin 8.7.3, Gradle 8.9) are the scaffold's, not necessarily yours.
The Gradle wrapper (`gradlew`, `gradlew.bat`, `gradle/wrapper/gradle-wrapper.jar`)
is a binary and is not included. Copy it from a liftoff project, or run
`gradle wrapper --gradle-version 8.9` once with a local Gradle install.

## Assets

All assets are optional. A missing file is replaced by a generated placeholder
and a warning is written to Logcat (tags `Assets` and `SoundEffects`), so the
game always runs. The bundled art, sounds and font are original and procedurally
generated (`tools/`, see [ASSETS.md](ASSETS.md)), except `sp_bg.png` (the title artwork), the faces (`enemy_head.png` and the three birds, cut from your
photos by `tools/make_faces.py`), the launcher icon, the font (ROGENZ demo, personal use only) and
the recordings you supplied. Put your own files with the same names in `android/assets/` to replace
them. Names, sizes and notes are in [ASSETS.md](ASSETS.md).

- **Lobby and splash background:** replace `android/assets/sp_bg.png` (drawn to cover the whole screen).
- **Pig face:** replace `android/assets/enemy_head.png` (square image; it is cropped to a circle at load time).
- **Bird faces:** replace the photos in `tools/source_art/` (`img2` red, `img3` yellow, `img4` blue) and run
  `python3 tools/make_faces.py`; it rewrites `bird.png`, `bird_yellow.png` and `bird_blue.png`.
- **Font:** `tools/fonts/ROGENZ-Demo.ttf` -> `python3 tools/generate_font.py` writes `font.fnt` + `font.png`.
- **App icon:** `tools/source_art/icon.jpg` -> `python3 tools/make_launcher_icon.py`.

(launcher and resources).

### Frame flow (in a level)

`GameScreen.render(delta)`:

1. `PhysicsWorld.update` steps Box2D with a fixed 1/60 s timestep (frame time
   clamped to 0.25 s). Before each step every entity stores its previous state.
   The contact listener only records impulses.
2. `DamageSystem.update` turns the recorded impulses into damage and destroys
   bodies, now that the world is not stepping.
3. `GameStateManager.update` retires and spawns birds and decides win or lose.
4. Every entity computes its draw position between its previous and current
   physics state (`PhysicsWorld.alpha`), so motion is smooth on 90/120 Hz screens.
5. `GameRenderer` draws the world in meters, then `Hud` draws the overlay in a
   separate virtual screen (at least 1280x720).

## Design decisions

- **Meters everywhere.** The physics world is 23.4 x 10.8 m. Pixels never enter the physics code.
- **Fullscreen without bars.** Both the world and the UI use an `ExtendViewport`: the picture fills the
  whole display and a wider screen just shows more scenery to the sides. The Android theme and launcher
  hide the system bars and draw under camera cut-outs; UI that sits near an edge respects the safe insets.
- **Interpolated rendering.** Physics runs at a fixed 60 Hz; sprites are drawn at positions
  interpolated between the last two steps (`Entity.renderX/Y/Angle`).
- **Tiled, not stretched, blocks.** Block textures repeat at a fixed size per meter along the
  block's long side; damage is shown with crack overlays.
- **Aiming preview = the real physics.** `AimPreview` integrates gravity and damping with the
  same fixed step Box2D uses, from the same launch velocity.
- **No body changes during the step.** Contact callbacks never touch health or bodies. They write to
  `ImpactQueue`; bodies are destroyed in `DamageSystem` and `GameStateManager` after the step.
- **Impulse-based damage.** `damage = max(0, impulse - threshold) * factor`, per material. All numbers
  live in `DamageConfig` and `Material`. A one-second grace period after loading ignores the tower settling.
- **Static while held.** The bird is a static body until released, so it needs no joints.
- **Retry = new `GameScreen`.** `hide()` disposes the old world and renderer, so no body or listener
  survives an attempt.
- **Events, not dependencies.** Game logic reports `GameEvents`; `SoundEffects` implements it.
- **One place for audio settings.** `AngryBirds.setMusicOn` / `setSoundOn` (mute) and `setMusicVolume` /
  `setSoundVolume` (slider) save the choice in `Settings` and apply it to `MusicPlayer` / `SoundEffects`
  (`enabled`, `volumeScale`); no screen touches the audio classes for this. All four values are restored at start-up.
  Muting keeps the volume, so unmuting returns to the same level.
- **Stars from score.** `ScoreManager.starsFor` maps the final score to 0-3 stars. `Assets.starOnFor` gives the
  colors by position (bronze, gold, silver): the result panel and the tower doors use them, so the gold star is the
  middle one. The result stars appear in the order bronze, silver, gold (`RESULT_STAR_RANK` in `Hud.kt`). Only the
  in-game score bar shows bronze, silver, gold from left to right (`SCORE_BAR_STAR_STYLE`).
- **Bitmap font and layouts.** A `GlyphLayout` is only valid at the font scale it was made with, so every
  text is drawn with the same size factor it was laid out with.
- **Placeholders are real textures.** They are backed by managed pixmaps, so they survive an Android GL
  context loss like file textures.

## Tuning notes

| What | Where |
|---|---|
| Launch power, max pull, minimum pull, grab radius, anchor | `systems/SlingshotConfig.kt` |
| Damage thresholds and factors, TNT blast radii, settle grace time | `entities/DamageConfig.kt` |
| Block density, friction, HP | `entities/Block.kt` (`Material`) |
| Pig HP and size | `entities/Enemy.kt` |
| Bird rest/timeout, abilities, win/lose settle times | `systems/GameStateManager.kt` |
| Points, bird bonus, star thresholds, score bar maximum | `systems/ScoreManager.kt` |
| Level layout and bird order | `level/Levels.kt` (generate with `tools/generate_level.py`) |
| Gravity, fixed step, iterations | `world/Units.kt` |
| Hit sound thresholds and volume | `audio/SoundEffects.kt` |
| Music volumes and fade speed | `audio/MusicPlayer.kt` |
| Default volume, volume restored when unmuting at 0% | `systems/Settings.kt` |
| Pause panel layout and volume rows | `render/Hud.kt` |
| Slider look and size | `render/VolumeSlider.kt` |
| Lobby layout, animation and settings panel | `screens/MenuScreen.kt` |
| Pig bar, score bar, counter, star order (score bar: `SCORE_BAR_STAR_STYLE`) | `render/Hud.kt`, `assets/Assets.kt` |

Rules of thumb: more `POWER` means a faster, flatter shot. Lower `DamageProfile.threshold` or higher
`factor` makes the structures more fragile. To inspect collisions, tap the top-right corner during play.

## Known limitations

- One playable level; the other 13 doors of the tower are locked. `LevelData` is hardcoded Kotlin
  (generated), not loaded from JSON.
- No automated tests; verification is the manual checklist in [docs/TESTING.md](docs/TESTING.md).
- Not implemented: follow camera, vibration, leaderboard backend.
- Very large `enemy_head.png` files (above about 2048 px) are decoded fully before cropping and may
  use a lot of memory.
